    Routines¶

    The routine model, its policy, the schedule vocabulary and the functions over them. See Routines for the narrative. Everything here but run_agent_spec imports from django_spec_agents.routines.

    AbstractRoutine¶

    AbstractRoutine ¶

    Bases: Model

    A stored prompt for one agent, its schedule and its principal, the user who created it.

    The consumer subclasses it with its tenant relation and returns its policy from routine_policy(). next_fire_at is None while the routine is disabled.

    thread_waits classmethod ¶

    thread_waits(thread_id: str, principal: Any) -> bool

    Whether the last firing's thread still waits on a person; the host reads its run store.

    Return True while principal's thread thread_id is parked on an approval or a question, and the sweep skips the next firing rather than start another beside it. The default, False, never skips, so a host without a run store fires on schedule.

    RoutineDraft¶

    RoutineDraft dataclass ¶

    What a create or an update writes to a routine.

    preapproved_tools names the tools to grant; the write pins each new name's destructive flag through the agent's guard, and keeps the flag already pinned for a name it held.

    WeeklySchedule¶

    WeeklySchedule dataclass ¶

    Fire on each of weekdays (0 is Monday, 6 is Sunday) at the local wall time at.

    at is a naive time to the minute, read in the routine's timezone. A time a DST change skips fires at the same instant shifted by the gap, and a time it repeats fires once, at its first occurrence. Raises InvalidRoutineError for no weekday, a weekday outside 0 to 6, an aware at, or one with seconds.

    IntervalSchedule¶

    IntervalSchedule dataclass ¶

    Fire every every_hours hours of elapsed time, whatever the timezone's DST changes.

    Raises InvalidRoutineError for an interval shorter than MIN_ROUTINE_INTERVAL or longer than MAX_ROUTINE_INTERVAL.

    PreapprovedTool¶

    PreapprovedTool dataclass ¶

    A tool a routine's firings may call without parking, and whether it was gated when granted.

    destructive is the guard's requires_approval answer at grant time, kept as it was: a tool granted while ungated and gated later is not covered, and its call parks.

    next_fire_at¶

    next_fire_at ¶

    next_fire_at ¶

    next_fire_at(schedule: Schedule, timezone: ZoneInfo, after: datetime) -> datetime

    The first firing of schedule strictly after the aware after, in UTC.

    A weekly schedule reads its wall time in timezone: a time a DST change skips fires shifted by the gap, and a repeated time fires at its first occurrence only. An interval adds elapsed hours to after, so pass the firing just claimed, not the sweep's clock, or every late sweep pushes the cadence later. Raises ValueError for a naive after.

    parse_schedule¶

    parse_schedule ¶

    parse_schedule ¶

    parse_schedule(data: object) -> Schedule

    Read a stored or submitted schedule; raises InvalidRoutineError outside the vocabulary.

    The vocabulary is closed: {"kind": "weekly", "weekdays": [0, ...], "at": "HH:MM"} or {"kind": "interval", "every_hours": N}, and no other key. There are no cron strings.

    create_routine¶

    create_routine ¶

    create_routine ¶

    create_routine(
    policy: RoutinePolicy[RoutineT],
    request: HttpRequest,
    draft: RoutineDraft,
    *,
    guard: ToolGuard,
    tool_defs: Iterable[ToolDefinition],
) -> RoutineT

    Create a routine from draft in the scope policy.new_routine gives, run as the caller.

    The caller is the principal and created_by, and must be authenticated. Each granted tool is pinned through guard against tool_defs, the definitions the agent offers. Raises InvalidRoutineError for a broken rule, an unknown tool, or a caller who already holds MAX_ROUTINES_PER_USER routines, and RoutineWriteRefusedError when the policy refuses.

    update_routine¶

    update_routine ¶

    update_routine ¶

    update_routine(
    policy: RoutinePolicy[RoutineT],
    request: HttpRequest,
    routine: RoutineT,
    draft: RoutineDraft,
    *,
    guard: ToolGuard,
    tool_defs: Iterable[ToolDefinition],
) -> RoutineT

    Replace routine's fields with draft's, if can_write admits it; the principal stays.

    Every granted name must be a tool in tool_defs. While the agent stays the same, a tool the routine already granted keeps the flag pinned when it was granted, so an edit never re-approves a tool declared destructive since; a new name, or every name when the agent changes, is pinned now. next_fire_at moves only when the schedule, the timezone or enabled changes. can_write is asked of the row as stored and of the result; a refusal leaves routine as it was. Raises InvalidRoutineError for a broken rule or an unknown tool, and RoutineWriteRefusedError when the policy refuses.

    pause_routine¶

    pause_routine ¶

    pause_routine ¶

    pause_routine(
    policy: RoutinePolicy[RoutineT], request: HttpRequest, routine: RoutineT
) -> RoutineT

    Disable routine, if can_write admits it, keeping its prompt, schedule and grant.

    next_fire_at clears, so the sweep skips it until an update enables it again. can_write is asked of the row as stored and of the result; a refusal leaves routine as it was and raises RoutineWriteRefusedError.

    list_routines¶

    list_routines ¶

    list_routines ¶

    list_routines(policy: RoutinePolicy[RoutineT], request: HttpRequest) -> list[RoutineT]

    The routines the caller may see whose principal is the caller, disabled ones too.

    A routine the policy shows the caller but that runs as someone else, a teammate's, is left out; an anonymous caller has none.

    authoring_toolset¶

    authoring_toolset ¶

    authoring_toolset ¶

    authoring_toolset(
    policy: RoutinePolicy[Any], request: HttpRequest, *, agent_id: str, guard: ToolGuard
) -> FunctionToolset[Any]

    The routine tools for a run on the agent agent_id, as the user request carries.

    list_routines lists the caller's own routines. create_routine, update_routine and pause_routine always ask first, whatever guard exempts or requires: each is marked with SELF_GATED_METADATA_KEY and raises ApprovalRequired with an x-confirm card that names the routine's principal, prompt, schedule, timezone and every pre-approved tool, a destructive one named as such. GrantedApprovals and ReviewedApprovals leave them to the person (NEVER_AUTO_APPROVED); a project's own HandleDeferredToolCalls must too. An approval that edits the arguments writes nothing: the model is told to call again, so the edited routine gets a card of its own. A routine's principal is always the caller, its agent always agent_id, and an update or a pause reaches only a routine whose principal is the caller; every write still goes through the policy's can_write. Each granted name is pinned through guard against the run's own tools, client tools and these four left out. Compose it into a browser run only: a routine's firing hides these tools, and a headless run has nobody to show the card to.

    due_routines¶

    due_routines ¶

    due_routines ¶

    due_routines(
    model: type[RoutineT],
    sweep: RoutineSweep,
    *,
    now: datetime | None = None,
    limit: int = 100,
) -> SweepResult

    Fire every enabled routine of model whose next_fire_at has come, at most limit of them.

    The due rows are claimed in one query with select_for_update(skip_locked=True), so a second sweep running at the same time passes over them, and each is moved to its next firing in the same transaction that enqueues it: a sweep run twice fires once. The queue sends what was enqueued when that transaction commits. Run it on a database that honours skip_locked, such as PostgreSQL; SQLite ignores the lock.

    Each claimed routine, in this order:

    • its last firing lapsed (its worker died, or its principal or spec went away): the routine is paused, enabled off and paused_reason naming the run, and last_run_id is cleared so the routine fires again once turned back on;
    • it is later than its grace (sweep.grace, by default one schedule period): it is skipped, logged and counted as missed, so downtime runs a missed firing at most once;
    • its last firing is still pending, or the model's thread_waits says its thread still waits on a person: it is skipped;
    • the policy's can_run refuses its principal: it is paused with the reason;
    • otherwise the spec sweep.spec_name is enqueued as the principal with {"routine_id": pk, "thread_id": <a fresh uuid hex>}; the firing spec runs into that thread, which becomes last_thread_id, and the run id the queue returns last_run_id.

    However late the sweep, next_fire_at moves to the first firing after now, stepped from the one claimed. A routine whose handling raises is rolled back, logged and left for the next sweep, and the others go on.

    RoutineSweep¶

    RoutineSweep dataclass ¶

    What run_due_routines sweeps a routine model with: the queue, the spec it enqueues.

    spec_name is the name the queue's registry holds the firing spec under. grace is how late a missed firing may still run; None is one schedule period, read at the missed firing.

    SweepResult¶

    SweepResult dataclass ¶

    What one sweep did with each routine it claimed, by primary key.

    fired were enqueued; missed were past their grace and skipped; busy were skipped because the last firing is still queued or running, or its thread still waits; paused were turned off; failed raised, were left as they were for the next sweep, and were logged.

    preapproved_exempt ¶

    run_agent_spec¶

    Needs the [specs] extra, so it is imported from its module, django_spec_agents.routines.run_agent_spec, never from the package.

    run_agent_spec ¶

    run_agent_spec(
    model: type[AbstractRoutine], runner: HeadlessRunner, *, timeout: float
) -> ServiceSpec

    The kernel spec a routine's firing runs: model's routine, to its first stop.

    Register it in the queue's registry under the RoutineSweep's spec_name. It takes the sweep's {"routine_id", "thread_id"} and runs the routine's prompt on its agent, as its principal, into that thread, within timeout seconds. It presents the RunStop: completed, waiting when a gated call parked the thread, timed_out or error, so a parked firing ends SUCCEEDED with waiting and the thread's approval card takes it from there.

    Its permission check refuses a routine that does not exist, a caller who is not the routine's principal, and a routine the model's policy can_run refuses, at enqueue and again on the worker, so a routine disabled in between lapses its firing.

    The run gets GrantedApprovals over preapproved_exempt, marked routine:<pk>: a pre-approved tool that was gated when granted runs without parking, its result marked, and the kernel's own authorization still runs on the call. Any other gated call parks. Its first request's metadata carries the grant under ROUTINE_GRANT_KEY, and the routine-authoring tools, ROUTINE_AUTHORING_TOOLS, are not offered.

    It declares atomic=False, so no transaction spans the run, and idempotent=False, so a worker that dies mid-run is not run again: the queue reports the firing lapsed.

    Limits¶

    MIN_ROUTINE_INTERVAL (an hour) and MAX_ROUTINE_INTERVAL (366 days) bound an interval. MAX_ROUTINES_PER_USER (25) caps a principal's routines. MAX_ROUTINE_NAME_LENGTH, MAX_ROUTINE_AGENT_ID_LENGTH and MAX_ROUTINE_PROMPT_LENGTH bound a draft's text.
